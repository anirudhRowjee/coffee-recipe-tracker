Simplification pass

1 · What feels complex

Ranked by how much each one would stop a new person. Brew mode is the exception: it's already the clearest screen, and it's the model for everything else.

Already right

Brew mode explains itself

Each number sits in a labelled tile that says what it's for: "15 g, beans to weigh", "93 °C, kettle set to", "22 on the Timemore". There's one big button, and the next step is always on screen. The rest of the app should copy this: say what a number is for, show one main action, and keep the rest a tap away.

Highest impact

Getting started is a scavenger hunt

  • The welcome popup says "go to Manage". On Manage, the setup forms sit below Lifetime savings (₹0), Brewing streak (0 days) and the brew-mode switches, all of which mean nothing yet.
  • Adding a coffee takes two forms: "+ Add Bean" (five fields), then "+ Add Bag" inside the new row (seven fields, including frozen date and low-stock alert).
  • Brewer and grinder are both required, so someone using pre-ground coffee has to invent a grinder called "No Grinder". The popup actually tells them to.
  • Back on Brew, there's no progress and no "next": just three empty pickers. After dismissing the popup there's no way to get the steps back.
  • The first recipe form asks for a dose, water, temperature and grind with no starting values and no example.
Highest impact

The recipe card does nine jobs at once

One card on Brew shows the recipe, starts brew mode, offers three share links and "Tidy into steps", plans the next brew (a note plus a suggested change), shows the recommendation, then opens into Log a brew (with past brews), Create variation (with change history), New named recipe, and Rename or delete. On a phone that's four screens of scrolling, and the main action (▶ Brew) looks the same size as everything else.

Highest impact

"Create variation" asks for the same change three times, then does something you can't see

  • To change the temperature you edit the Temp field, pick "Temp" under "What changed", and type "+3" under "Delta amount". The server already knows which field changed (validate_one_parameter_delta works it out), but it saves the dropdown's answer, so the two can disagree.
  • Saving a variation also logs a brew of the new version, which takes the dose out of the bag, even if the tasting notes are empty. Nothing on the form says so.
  • The form repeats Log a brew's fields (tasting notes, rating, recommend next change, why), so there are two places to log a brew, and they behave differently.
  • The one-change rule is the heart of the app, but people only find out about it from an error page ("Must change exactly one parameter. Changed: ['dose_g', 'temp_c']").

High

Browse and Manage overlap, and Manage holds five things

  • Manage covers stats (savings, streak), settings (brew mode), stock (beans and bags), gear (brewers and grinders) and admin (users). A new person looking for "my coffees" has two pages to check.
  • Browse shows bags and history for each coffee, but bags are added and edited on Manage.
  • "Schedule next brew" on Browse and "Plan for next time" on Brew are the same thing, with different forms.
Medium

Three pickers before anything shows

Unless a brew is planned, Brew shows a greeting and three pickers (bag in a popup, then brewer and grinder dropdowns) before any recipe appears. Most coffees are brewed with the same gear each time, so the app could remember the last gear per coffee and open on the coffee you brewed last.

Small, quick fixes

Paper cuts

  • "ID 12" on every coffee on Browse and Manage: a database number nobody needs.
  • On Browse, "Last brewed Jun 14" is really the date that version was saved, not the last brew.
  • Browse is in alphabetical order, so "729 Grams Swiss Water Decaf" comes first instead of the coffee you're drinking now.
  • "15.0 g", "240.0 ml", "93.0 °C": trailing ".0" everywhere outside brew mode.
  • The footer says "Lightweight HTMX app · SQLite backend", which is a note for developers.
  • Manage's coffee rows wrap into tall narrow columns on a phone (the name, then "ID 12 · Guatemala · Medium-Dark · 1 bag" squeezed beside it).
  • "Share as text" and "Download .md": friends won't know what .md is.
  • "Log a brew" opens on its own only when a brew is planned, so the same card looks different from day to day.

2 · Mockups for iPhone and iPad

Designed for touch first: your iPhone in Bluefy, and the iPad in landscape. The Kindle is no longer a target, so these use bottom sheets, popovers and steppers freely. They keep what matters: one change per version, and existing recipes stay exactly as they are. The ? buttons are the real help pattern from section 3; tap them.

Thumb firstThe main action sits at the bottom of the screen, above a bottom tab bar. Nothing important is only reachable at the top.
Sheets, not long formsEvery secondary task opens in a bottom sheet (phone) or a popover (iPad) over the page you're on, then closes. Nothing grows the page by four screens.
Tap, don't typeSteppers, chips and stars instead of number fields, so no ± button and no keyboard while holding a kettle.
Big targetsEvery control is at least 44 points. The tip "?" is drawn small but its hit area is 44.
iPad gets two columnsLandscape iPad shows the list and the detail side by side, with a side rail for navigation. No stretched phone layout.
Brew mode is the modelLabelled tiles that say what each number is for, one big button, the next thing always visible.

A · Brew: one coffee, one big button

A2 · Brew when nothing is picked

"Up next" is chosen in this order: the brew you planned; otherwise the coffee you brewed most recently that still has coffee left, with the gear and recipe you used; otherwise nothing, and Brew asks. You also get here from "Pick another coffee".

B · Picking a coffee: a sheet, recent first

C · Try a change: tap the number you want to change

The one-change rule becomes something you can see. Tap a tile and the other three lock. There's nothing to explain in an error, because you can't change two things.

C2 · Changing the method steps

Variations haven't gone; they're what Try a change makes, now called versions. The method is one of the five things you can pick, exactly as "Recipe text" is today. After brewing you get here two ways: pick Change steps under Next time in the log sheet (it opens here next time), or tap Try a change → Method straight away.

Methods written as free text (from before the step editor) open as plain text with a "Tidy into steps" button, as they do now; nothing is converted unless you ask.

C3 · Recipes and their versions

Two ideas, kept apart. A recipe is one approach to a coffee with some gear; you can keep several. Versions are the steps of tuning one recipe; each is one change from the one before, and Brew always uses the newest.

4:6 sweetv1temp +2 →v2grind +1 →v3temp +3 →v4 · current
Defaultv1dose −1 →v2… →v5 · current

You reach a recipe's versions from its card ("version 4" is a link), from Coffees, or from "History" on the iPad's Brew page.

Decided

No jumping back to an older version. Every move is one change: "Undo the last change" puts the previous value back as a new version, and going further back is another undo or another change. If a version doesn't work, its one change tells you what to undo or adjust, and the history stays a clean list of single changes.

D · Logging a cup: right after Stop, all taps

D2 · Changing the steps right after Stop

Pick Change steps in the log sheet and you choose: edit them now, or leave a note for next time. Editing now saves the cup against the version you just brewed, then saves the new steps as the next version. With a scale connected, each row shows what you actually did, and one tap copies it in.

E · Coffees: list and detail, one home per coffee

Browse plus the coffee half of Manage. Bags, recipes and history for a coffee all live on its page, and history reads "Temp 91 → 93 °C" (real values, as the share export already does) instead of temp_c 2.0.

F · First run: a checklist where you land

G · Settings: gear, stats and switches

Brewers and grinders get a home in Settings under "Your gear". You can also add one from anywhere you pick gear: the gear sheet on Brew (B) and the first-run checklist (F) both have a "+ Add" chip that opens the same sheet. Savings and streak move here too, so Brew stays about the next cup.

H · Fixing things afterwards

Cups and versions can both be corrected. Tasting notes, rating and "Next time" on any cup are always editable, because a cup tastes different once it cools. Versions can be corrected too, within the one-change rule.

I · Form checks that never lose your work

Today 17 checks happen only on the server, and a failure is a plain text page that throws away what you typed. In the new design the page catches problems as you go, and the server's checks stay as a backstop that brings you back to the form with everything still filled in.

Impossible by designSteppers have sensible limits (coffee 5–60 g, water 50–1,000 ml, temperature 70–100 °C, grind within the grinder's range), and Try a change locks the other tiles.
Say it beside the fieldProblems show in red under the field as soon as you leave it, never in a pop-up. Save stays greyed out and says why.
Warn, don't blockUnusual but possible things (a 1:25 ratio, a bag with less than a dose left, a roast date in the future) get an amber note and still save.
Nothing typed is lostTasting notes are saved as a draft on the device while you type, and come back after a reload or a dropped connection.
Undo, not "Are you sure?"Deleting a cup or a bag shows Undo for 10 seconds. Big deletes (a recipe with all its versions) still ask, and say what goes with them.
Server as a backstopIf the server still refuses something, the form comes back filled in, with the message beside the field, and the same checks run on both sides.
Also on the list
  • Which bag: a coffee with two open bags uses the oldest one that isn't frozen, shown as a chip you can switch.
  • Finishing a bag: when less than a dose is left, the log sheet offers "Mark this bag finished".
  • Gear with history: archive instead of delete, so old recipes stay readable.
  • Grind steppers: each grinder has a step size and range; settings like "2.5.0" fall back to text.
  • One Share sheet: a live preview with switches for curve, tasting note, story size and rating, instead of three links and a page that reloads for each extra.
  • Tidy into steps becomes a "Turn into steps" button inside the step editor.
  • Brew mode's bar only shows the buttons that apply right now (no Tare without a scale, no Reset beside Stop while brewing).

3 · Tooltips and help

Touch screens have no hover, so every tip opens on a tap. There are three layers:

Where the tips go, and what they say

First drafts, two sentences at most. ★ marks the places a new person reaches first.

Build: one Jinja macro, tip('grind'), renders the ? button and its popover. The copy lives in one tips.py dict, so the wording is easy to review and to test. Whether someone has seen the tour can live in localStorage (per device, no schema change) or in a small column on the user (follows them across devices).

4 · Order of work

Smallest and safest first. Each step ships and can be tried on its own; only the tour's "seen" flag might need a new column.

  1. Words and paper cutsRecipe / Version / Try a change / Next time / Up next; drop "ID", ".0" and the footer; real dates and "before → after" in history; coffees ordered by last brewed. Templates only.
  2. Fixing cupsEdit any cup's notes, rating, Next time and bag, "+ Cooled note", delete with Undo. A small, separate change that helps straight away; no schema change.
  3. Touch shellBottom tab bar on phones, side rail on a landscape iPad, a pinned main button, and one reusable sheet/popover component (a <dialog> that's a bottom sheet on a phone and a popover on an iPad). Update AGENTS.md: it still says Kindle first.
  4. Help layerThe tip macro with about 20 tips, the three-card tour, and the How this works sheet.
  5. Try a change by tapping a tileIncludes the step editor for the method (rows with − and + for grams, a time stepper, action chips). The server takes the changed setting and amount from the values, and saving no longer logs a brew quietly. The one-change rule is unchanged, just visible.
  6. Logging in a sheet after StopIncludes "Change steps": edit now (with the scale's actual times beside each row) or note it for next time. Stars, taste chips and Next time chips, in brew mode and from Brew. Same fields as today; old brews look the same.
  7. Brew page and coffee pickerThe Up next card with tiles, the two-column iPad layout, the recent-first picker, and gear remembered from the last brew.
  8. Versions pageOne line per version with ratings, compare two versions, "Brew it once" for an older version, and Undo the last change. No jumping back: further back is another undo. Fix the newest version (its own change and reason), delete a version with no cups.
  9. Form checksLimits on every stepper, inline messages, drafts of tasting notes, Undo for deletes, and server errors that come back to the filled-in form. Built into each step above as it lands, plus one pass over today's 17 server-only checks.
  10. Coffees, Settings and first runCoffees as list and detail (merging Browse); Settings for gear, brew mode, account and users; the first-run checklist with Pre-ground and starting recipes.
